Vision

What this is: the problem Forge exists to solve, who it's for, and what "working" means at beta — pulled from William's own Proposal and Final Requirements Spec, never invented.

The problem

Forge exists to break down the "barrier of intimidation and self-consciousness that keeps people from starting a fitness journey." That exact phrasing recurs verbatim across William's Proposal and his Final Requirements Spec — it is his own framing, not a paraphrase.

The Pre-Proposal states the same problem from the other direction: a disconnect that leads to self-consciousness and hesitancy to work out in public. The proposed fix is two-sided — features for planning workouts with others, plus community interaction tied to a person's actual physical gym.

SourceFraming
Elevator Pitch"A social fitness app aimed at making the gym less intimidating by helping people start their journey with friends and supportive communities."
Pre-ProposalDisconnect → self-consciousness → hesitancy to start. Solved by social planning + gym-tied community.

Mission

Quoted verbatim from the extraction of William's notes:

"To create a one-stop-shop for people to connect with their gym, trainer, and other gym goers in order to create a more inviting and connected experience for gym communities."

Note the mission statement still names a trainer product and a gym product alongside the consumer app — that is the earliest, least-formal framing of the idea. See the scope call below for how that narrowed.

Target users

Audience: adults 18 through the late 50s, with the majority being younger adults already active on social media. The Proposal names three concrete archetypes:

ArchetypeWhat they want from Forge
New gym attendeeHelp getting started — a less intimidating on-ramp to a fitness routine.
Consistent attendeeConnection — plan workouts with friends, stay accountable, share progress.
Gym (as an account type)Host its own community — post updates, run channels, manage membership.

No named individual stakeholders exist. The formal Proposal states plainly: "Stakeholders with Contact information: TBD."

Scope call — one app, not a company

PROPOSED Needs William's review — the single biggest open call in this brief.

Forge is one consumer app. It is not the three-product "AlpFit" business (consumer app + gym management software + trainer software) described in the earliest, undated brainstorm. That three-product vision appears exactly once and never resurfaces in any formal Proposal or Requirements doc — every later document scopes to a single Flutter app in which "gym" is an account type, not a separate product.

Direction of travel favors one app: ambition narrowed consistently as the notes got more formal. The trainer product and the gym-facing website are DEFERRED. This is tagged PROPOSED rather than decided because it is a question about what the product fundamentally is — that call belongs to William, and he was away when this brief was written.

What success looks like at beta

The only stakeholder record in the entire corpus is the "Bigger Rec Manager Meeting" note. It describes a beta group split into two roles, and Milestone 3 (rec center beta) is built directly on this arrangement:

RoleDefinition
The GymHosts its own community; communicates news and events to members.
The UserUses the app; participates in the host gym's community.

William's Final Requirements Spec sets a verification bar that doubles as the cleanest definition of "done" anywhere in the notes. Quoted verbatim:

"A requirement is considered fulfilled when the expected behavior occurs through the user interface and the resulting data is correctly saved and displayed across app restarts. A requirement will not be fulfilled if an action fails, produces incorrect or missing data, or does not persist across restarts."

Applied to the beta: success is five or more non-William users signing up, joining the rec center's community, and posting — with everything they did still there the next time they open the app.

Competitive position

William's own research found no direct competitor: "As far as I can find this specific type of app has not been created." Existing fitness apps focus on workout programming and meal planning, not on the social/community layer Forge is built around.

Stated honestly, this is a claim from one person's search, not a market study — there is no named competitor to contrast against, and no evidence of a broader competitive audit.
