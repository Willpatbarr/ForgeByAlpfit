Vision

What Forge is, who it serves, and how success is measured.

The thesis

Gym intimidation is a social problem, not a programming problem — so Forge does not sell you a better workout, it sells you people to do it with.

Author's own words, from the proposal: there is "a barrier of intimidation and self-consciousness that keeps people from starting a fitness journey", and "when we make genuine human connections we feel safer. If people feel safer they are more likely to try new things." Every scope call in these docs is downstream of that sentence.

Who it's for

Audience is adults 18 to late 50s, skewing to younger adults who already use social apps. The notes name three user categories; one of them is the gym itself.

  • The prospect — Signed up for a membership, or thinking about it. Has never been inside, and is the most intimidated person in the product. First five minutes: create account → search the gym by name → join its community → read the channel and see that these are normal people → message someone or accept an invite to a workout.   • The new attendee — Attends but wanders. Doesn't know what to do or who to ask. The proposal's "new gym attendee looking for help." First five minutes: create account → join gym → add the one or two people they already know → see their friends' upcoming workouts on the feed → ask to join one.

The differentiator

You join a gym's community before your first visit — so the first time you walk in, you already know someone.

This is the one idea that survives every era of the notes. It is in the earliest pre-proposal, it is in the proposal verbatim ("New attendees would also be able to go and join a gym's community before even attending the gym, getting to know people and asking questions so they feel more prepared for their first workout"), it is one of the four differentiators in the project summary, and it is the reason the gym account type exists at all.

What competitors doWhat Forge does
Fitness apps connect you to a program.Forge connects you to a room full of people.
Social apps connect you to people anywhere.Forge connects you to the people at your gym, at a specific hour.
Gym apps start working after you're a member and inside the building.Forge starts working before you've ever opened the door.

Everything else in the product — friends, calendar, invites, channels — exists to make that sentence true. When a feature doesn't serve it, that's the signal to cut the feature.

What Forge is deliberately not

The anti-scope is the author's own, stated in the proposal: "There are many apps that focus on delivering high quality programming or meal planning tools. This app would not try to enter that market, but merely touch on those."

Not a…Why notTrigger that would change it
Workout programming app Exercises describe a planned workout; they never prescribe one. Beta users ask "what should I do today" more than "who is going."
Meal planning / nutrition app Zero connection to the social thesis. Nothing currently on the table — treat as permanently out.
Trainer marketplace The trainer platform is cut (D4), not deferred. A gym asks Forge to sell training sessions on its behalf.
Gym management software The gym gets an account type inside Forge, not a product (D4). Three gyms ask for a roster export or desktop moderation.
  • Workout programming app — Exercises exist to describe a planned workout so a friend knows what they're joining — never to prescribe one. Competing on programming means competing with a dozen funded apps on their strongest ground. Even if beta users repeatedly ask "what should I do today" rather than "who is going today," the answer is a shared library, not a coach.
  • Meal planning / nutrition app — Zero connection to the social thesis. The author has the domain background (ESS 381) and still ruled it out. Nothing is currently on the table; treat this one as permanently out.
  • Trainer marketplace — The trainer platform was one of AlpFit's three ambitions and is cut (D4). No trainer role, account type, or feature exists anywhere in the code, and the trainer interview notes the proposal cites were never written down. It would reopen only if a gym asked Forge to sell training sessions on its behalf — a payments product, i.e. a different company.
  • Gym management software — Membership billing, check-ins, class booking, staff scheduling. The gym gets an account type inside Forge, not a separate product (D4). Building gym-side software means selling B2B to institutions with procurement cycles. Three gyms independently asking for a member roster export or a desktop moderation console would change it — and even then, the desktop trigger belongs to D3, not to a new product line.

Success looks like

These five metrics are my invention, not his. Both intake passes flag the same gap: the notes contain no success metrics of any kind. The only definition of done in the corpus is "demo-able" — a grade, not a product signal — and the only measures are per-session ("the user created or edited an event"). Everything below is tagged PROPOSED because you have never stated a number. Change any of them freely; the point is that there is one to change.
#MetricTargetWhy this oneMeasurable today?
SM1 Beta cohort size at the BYU-I Rec Center 30 members, plus the gym account itself Small enough to hand-recruit, big enough not to be a ghost town. Yes — a count
SM2 % of workout events with an invitee who accepted ≥ 40% The entire product thesis in one number. No — RSVP is never persisted
SM3 Week-2 retention ≥ 35% of sign-ups open the app in their second week Cheap, standard, and it catches the sign-up-and-vanish failure. Partial — countable by hand at n=30
SM4 Gyms onboarded with a live channel 3 gyms within six months of first external release One gym is a favor; three is a pattern. Yes — a count
SM5 New users who join a gym community in their first session ≥ 60% The differentiator only fires if joining a gym is the first act. Partial — nothing tracks "session"
  • SM1 — 30 members in the first cohort is small enough to hand-recruit, big enough that a gym channel isn't a ghost town. The rec-manager notes already ask for two beta cohorts — gym-side and user-side.
  • SM2 — This is the entire product thesis in one number. A workout nobody accepted is a note-to-self; Forge only worked if two people showed up. If exactly one number survives editing, keep this one. It is not measurable today: RSVP is never persisted (see Looks done, isn't), and fixing that is a prerequisite for measuring anything.
  • SM3 — Cheap, standard, and it catches the failure mode where everyone signs up at a table in the rec center lobby and nobody comes back. Only partially measurable: it needs analytics, which is deliberately not adopted yet, though it is countable by hand at n=30.
  • SM4 — One gym is a favor. Three is a pattern, and three is also the stated trigger for reopening monetization (D8).
  • SM5 — The differentiator only fires if joining a gym is the natural first act. If this number is low, onboarding — not the feature set — is the problem. Only partially measurable: derivable from joinedGymIds vs account age, but nothing tracks "session."

Note what is not here: downloads, DAU, and time-in-app. A social app for a single gym with 30 beta users cannot move those numbers, and chasing them would pull the product toward engagement mechanics that make gyms feel less welcoming, not more.

Current AlpFit scope

AlpFit was described in the earliest vision doc as three products, each usable "independently or together": a consumer app, gym software (website + mobile), and trainer software (website + mobile). That is not the plan any more.

  • Consumer mobile app (Forge) — ~16,300 lines of it already run. It is the only thing that exists.
  • Gym software — D4. Gyms get an account type inside Forge — already built, already the differentiator. A separate B2B product is a different company.
  • Trainer software — D4. No trainer role, account type, feature, or line of code exists anywhere. The account-type enum is member/gym only. See the deferred table — it is marked cut, not parked.

The brand name stays. The three-platform strategy does not. If you want the ambition back on the page, the honest place for it is a "someday" line in the decision log with a trigger, which is exactly where it now lives.

Where this product actually is

The app is substantially built and nowhere near shippable, and those are both true. Roughly 16,300 lines run against live Firestore across feed, calendar, events, goals, plans, DMs, friend requests, gym channels, and avatars. It also has zero tests, a world-readable user table (firestore.rules grants allow read: if true on /users, exposing every email and contact field unauthenticated), no moderation, no account deletion, no push, and com.example.* bundle ids that both app stores reject outright. Do not read "substantially built" as "nearly done."
  • The vision has been stable for two years. It does not need re-deciding — see decisions.html.
  • The highest-value engineering work is the surfaces that look finished and aren't — see features.html → Looks done, isn't.
  • The highest-severity item in the whole project is one line in firestore.rules — see the data model and the milestones.
