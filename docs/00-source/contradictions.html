1 · Three-platform AlpFit strategy LOCKED

A: AlpFit is three products — consumer app, gym software, trainer software — each "usable independently or together", and gym + trainer each get a website and mobile application. Consumer app can "connect and communicate with your trainer". AlpFit Mission Statement_Goals

B: no trainer role, trainer account type, or trainer feature exists in any later doc or anywhere in the code. Account type is user | gym only. Final Specs, Forge Initial Requirements, auth_service.dart

Resolution: consumer iOS/Android app only. Trainer platform and standalone gym software are cut, not deferred; the "website and mobile app" promises die with them. Road not taken: an AlpFit product suite. Per D4 and the settled scope statement.

2 · Gym account: core or "much later"? DECIDED

A: "eventually as a stretch goal the actual management of a fitness center would also be a user. That would come much later though." Pre-Proposal

B: gym registration type at signup AUTH_IMPLEMENTATION_CONTEXT.md; a whole "Gyms only" requirements block Forge Initial Requirements; gym channels as a headline feature Forge Proposal; a gym-side beta cohort Bigger Rec Manager Meeting; and it is built — gym accounts, channels, posts, member rosters, kicks.

Resolution: the gym account type stays and is the differentiator. Pre-Proposal is the earliest doc in the set; every later artifact and the code overrule it. Per D4.

3 · Target platforms DECIDED

A: Flutter chosen because it "will allow me to easily deploy to mobile, desktop, and web clients without having to create extra code"; targets listed as iOS/Android, desktop, web. Forge Proposal, PROJECT_SUMMARY.md

B: only web is deploy-configured (Firebase Hosting → build/web). Linux throws UnsupportedError in firebase_options.dart. macOS/Windows/Linux scaffolds are untested and unreachable. No user ever asked for desktop. code

Resolution: iOS + Android only. The other targets exist because Flutter generates them, not because anyone wanted them. Desktop/web DEFERRED. Per D3.

Revisit when: a gym admin asks to moderate from a desktop browser.

4 · Performance tracking & programming scope PROPOSED needs your call

A: "database of movements", "track maxes on each movement", and "insights on when you've done a given movement better and what has impacted it (e.g. to a certain song, certain time, or before or after another movement)". AlpFit Overview

B: load-bearing anti-scope: "There are many apps that focus on delivering high quality programming or meal planning tools. This app would not try to enter that market, but merely touch on those." Forge Proposal — and none of the three features appear in any later doc or anywhere in the code. Exercise names are free text; there is no movement entity and no PR record.

Resolution: anti-scope holds. Forge plans workouts socially; it does not prescribe programming or analyse performance. Max tracking, movement insights, and the movement catalog stay cut. Road not taken: a movement catalog with PR history — a genuinely different product. Recommended default: keep cut.

Revisit when: you decide Forge tracks performance, not just plans it.

5 · Live workout session screen PROPOSED needs your call

B: no such screen appears in AlpFit Mission, Forge Initial Requirements, Final Specs, or the code. Workouts are only planned and scheduled, never executed.

Resolution: out of scope for v1. This is the single largest unattributed scope in the corpus — it has a spec and no requirement lineage. It is an execution/tracking feature and pulls against the social thesis (record 4). Road not taken: Forge as a during-the-workout companion. Recommended default: stay cut.

Revisit when: it turns out to be your newest thinking rather than an abandoned idea.

6 · Gym discovery PROPOSED needs your call

Resolution: search only. The map was silently dropped — never deferred, never argued against. We are making the drop explicit rather than reviving it: it needs geo data the product does not collect. Road not taken: map-based gym discovery as the hook. Recommended default: keep cut.

12 · Goals: must-have or stretch? DECIDED

A: "create goals" is listed under Stretch requirements Final Specs. And Goals appear in no requirements doc at all — not in IR's five pages, not in the Proposal's seven major features, not in PROJECT_SUMMARY's feature list.

B: a dedicated requirements doc Goal Requirements, a full class design Forge_Goal_Class_Design, a live Goals widget on the feed grid Feed Page Widget Modules, and ~1,000 lines of the app's most polished code — models/goal.dart, goals_service.dart, goal_creator_sheet.dart, feed check-in rings.

Resolution: must-have. Direction of travel wins: the Final Specs is the later document, but every goal artifact post-dates it in activity. Road not taken: goals as a post-launch add-on. Per D2.

13 · How many event types? DECIDED

A: three: "create events of three types: workout, goal, and basic event". AlpFit Mission

B: two: basic and workout. Forge Initial Requirements, Final Specs. Code: eventType ∈ 'basic' | 'workout', and goals live in their own collection. events_service.dart

Resolution: two event types. Goals are their own entity, not an event type — which is the right call anyway, since a goal is a period counter and an event is an appointment (see record 31).

14 · Calendar view modes DECIDED

A: three competing lists: "month, week, or day" AlpFit Mission; "year, month, multi, day" Forge Initial Requirements, PROJECT_SUMMARY.md; "daily, multi day, and monthly" Final Specs

B: code ships exactly three: Day, Multi = a fixed 4 days (calendar_page.dart:594), and Month. No year view. No true week view. calendar_page.dart

Resolution: code wins: Day / Multi(4) / Month. Year view is cut — a year grid of gym sessions is unreadable. A true 7-day week view is DEFERRED. Do not describe "week" as shipped.

Revisit when: two beta testers ask for a week view.

15 · Calendar source filtering DECIDED

A: an explicit requirement: "filter which event sources are shown", alongside "show which events came from where (gyms, friends, self)". Forge Initial Requirements, PROJECT_SUMMARY.md. Survives in the Final Specs only as an assumed clause — "provided the event owner is not filtered out in the calendar view".

B: the filter popup is entirely fake: hardcoded 'You', 'Friend 1', 'Ethan', 'Gym 1' at calendar_page.dart:44-56, and _filterStates is never read by any query.

Resolution: the feature does not exist; the control that claims it does is the clearest doc-vs-code lie in the repo. Keep the requirement and wire it (T1). A fake control in a shipping screen is worse than no control — if it isn't wired before a beta, delete the popup.

16 · Create an event by tapping a time DECIDED

A: "start creating an event by clicking on a time of day" — the calendar is an entry point to the Event Creator. Forge Initial Requirements, PROJECT_SUMMARY.md

B: not implemented. Tapping empty grid space does nothing. calendar_page.dart

Resolution: keep the requirement — it is the natural gesture and it is cheap. T2, after the filter (record 15) and the fake-detail-sheet stub. Not a scope change, just unbuilt.

17 · Workout co-editing PROPOSED needs your call

A: "invite friends to edit workout" — shared authorship of a workout event. Forge Initial Requirements

B: absent from the Final Specs, which only has "invite friends to either kind of event" — participation, not editing. And architecturally impossible in the code: allow write: if request.auth.uid == userId means only the owner can ever edit. firestore.rules

Resolution: cut. It needs a shared-ownership model the data layer does not have (events are subcollections of one user), so it is not a small addition. The drop looks unintentional, which is why it is flagged. Road not taken: collaboratively authored workouts. Recommended default: stay cut; invitees participate, the owner edits.

Revisit when: two users ask to build one workout together.

18 · Friend groups PROPOSED needs your call

A: "create and join groups of other users to interact with" — a third social tier beyond friends and gyms, listed as a Social page requirement. Forge Initial Requirements

B: demoted to Stretch — "create groups of friends to share events and posts with" Final Specs — and then not implemented at all: no group entity, no collection, no rules, no index, no UI. code

Resolution: cut. A group is a third membership model on top of friends and gyms, and the gym channel already covers "a set of people who talk to each other". Road not taken: friend groups as private mini-communities. Recommended default: keep cut.

Revisit when: a gym asks for sub-communities, or friends ask to plan in a fixed crew.

19 · User-authored posts DECIDED

A: users "post to communities" AlpFit Mission; "users can interact with and post on certain channels of a gym's community" AlpFit Overview, Forge Proposal

B: "make posts for friends to see" is Stretch Final Specs. Code: gym channel posts exist and work; there is no personal post entity at all, so the feed's "updates from friends" actually means "your friend scheduled something". updates_service.dart

Resolution: split the collision — these were never the same feature. Community posting is must-have and built. Friend-directed personal posts stay cut: a personal timeline is a different product and would multiply the moderation surface. The feed must stop implying friends post.

20 · Notifications & push PROPOSED needs your call

A: push-driven refresh "to avoid having to reopen the app each time", workout reminders, attendance confirmation, buddy-cancellation alerts AlpFit Overview; lock-screen goal check-in (stretch) and a goal-archival notification Goal Requirements

B: zero notification requirements in the Final Specs, must-have or stretch. None in any senior-project doc either. push_service.dart is 0 bytes — invites, DMs, and friend requests are completely silent.

Resolution: no push in v1. This is the largest silent drop with a live product cost: a social scheduling app where you only learn about an invite by opening the app. FCM is free when adopted. Road not taken: notification-driven engagement from day one. Recommended default: no push before the beta.

Revisit when: retention is the problem worth solving, or a tester says they missed an invite.

21 · Themes DECIDED

A: "adjust user settings and themes" listed under the Settings page. AlpFit Mission

B: "edit color theme of app" is Stretch Final Specs. Code: one hardcoded Color(0xFF4D7CFF) re-declared in at least six files, and core/constants/colors.dart is empty.

Resolution: cut. You cannot theme an app that has no color constant. The real work hiding behind this record is consolidating the six copies of forge blue into one place — do that instead.

22 · Feed widget editability DEFERRED

A: "edit the widgets available on the feed page" is Stretch. Final Specs

B: a concrete 5×6 grid with per-widget position/size, designed precisely so "we can easily move the widgets around and resize them" — a later, implementation-grade doc. Feed Page Widget Modules. But the code ships a fixed hand-built layout and buildBasicFeedWidget is dead code. feed_page.dart:92

Resolution: the grid doc is later and more considered, but it did not survive into the code, and its own coordinates do not close (Next Workout p=0:0 h=3 w=4, Goals p=0:2 h=2 w=4, Friends p=4:0 h=4 w=1 overlap or exceed 5×6 under either reading of row:col). Confirm the coordinate convention before building anything on it.

Revisit when: a second widget type is wanted.

23 · Add-existing-event / reusable event backlog DECIDED

A: "adding an event prompts you for a new event or add existing event"; the Create page holds "created events to edit or add to calendar" — a created-but-unscheduled backlog. AlpFit Mission

B: event creation ends at "user taps Create Event, at which point the event is saved" — no template or backlog concept. Final Specs

Resolution: the idea survived, under different names: Plans materialize repeated events and the Create hub browses your existing workouts/plans/goals to reuse. Both are shipped. The Mission-era wording is superseded, not dropped. Road not taken: an explicit unscheduled-event backlog.

24 · Gym community page "and subpages" DECIDED

A: gyms can "create and manage community page and subpages" — a must-have Gyms-only requirement. Forge Initial Requirements, Final Specs, PROJECT_SUMMARY.md

B: code: a flat channelNames array on the gym's user doc, with channelName as a string field on each post. No subpages. And the community page's main pane is an empty state — there is no gym feed. social_page.dart, channel_posts_service.dart

Resolution: "subpages" means channels — same concept, older word. One level, no nesting. The genuine gap is the empty community pane: a gym community page that shows nothing is a T1 defect, not a missing stretch feature.

25 · Gym membership approval DECIDED

A: gyms can "remove from or accept users into managed community"; joining "may be instant or require approval, depending on the gym's configuration". Forge Initial Requirements, Final Specs

B: code: addGym joins instantly, unconditionally. Only the kick half exists (gym removes member). There is no request, no queue, no approval state. gyms_service.dart

Resolution: instant join for v1 — it matches the thesis (join before your first visit, with no gatekeeper). Approval is DEFERRED. Remove the "accept users into" claim from the requirement list until then.

Revisit when: a gym asks to gate its community.

26 · Moderation DECIDED

A: three must-have requirements: gyms "moderate posts and interactions", "remove from or accept users into a community", plus the hedge "community management and moderation workflows are not yet fully defined". Forge Initial Requirements, Final Specs

B: moderation_service.dart is 0 bytes. There is no reporting, no blocking, no hiding, no content filtering. The only control is delete — and isCurrentUserGymOwner is literally uid == gymUid, so a post's own author cannot delete their own post. channel_posts_service.dart:357

Resolution: moderation is T1 and blocking, not a stretch nicety: App Store guideline 1.2 requires report/block for UGC apps. Fix the owner check (author OR gym), then add report + block. The "not yet fully defined" hedge is now defined by this record.

27 · Typography DECIDED

A: "font should be Poppins with no bold or styled text" — the only typography directive in the entire corpus, and it sits on an orphan spec. Workout Page Requirements Spec

B: no typography direction anywhere else. Goal Requirements was authored in RobotoMono. Code: Poppins is the app-wide font app/app.dart, used with weights in app_text_styles.dart.

Resolution: Poppins is the app font — the code already agrees. The "no bold or styled text" clause was scoped to one screen that does not exist (record 5) and is dropped. Not a conflict so much as an unpropagated rule that turned out to be half-right.

28 · Goal check-ins: scheduled or not DECIDED

A: opening narrative: users "set a recurring set of days and times that they go and checkup on the goal". Goal Requirements

B: same file, answers #3 and #5: "Originally I was thinking they should be but I've changed my mind… The more important time frame will be the reset period"; "checkupFrequency can be scrapped… I am scrapping scheduled checkups." Goal Requirements

Resolution: this is him changing his mind mid-document, not a real disagreement. Latest position is current: period-based check-in counts, no schedule. Forge_Goal_Class_Design confirms by omitting any schedule, and the code implements it.

29 · Goal class: first sketch vs final design DECIDED

A: first sketch: checkupFrequency: Int, resetFrequency: Float? (an arbitrary numeric period), nextCheckupStatus: Bool, checkupHistory, with generic set__/get__. Goal Requirements

B: final: requiredCheckupsPerPeriod: int, resetPeriod: ResetPeriod (enum: daily/weekly/biweekly/monthly), currentCheckupsCompleted, periodHistory: List<GoalPeriodRecord>. Forge_Goal_Class_Design

Resolution: final design. It is explicitly the resolution of the sketch — the same file's 18 answers scrap the fields listed above them — and it shipped near-verbatim as lib/models/goal.dart. Known cost, accept it: the enum cannot express an arbitrary period; "6 workouts every 2 weeks" survives only because biweekly exists.

30 · Where goal business logic lives DECIDED

A: the Goal class owns checkup() and a private reset(). Goal Requirements

B: "the Goal class should be a DATA MODEL only" — period resetting and archiving of expired goals belong in a separate GoalService. Forge_Goal_Class_Design

Resolution: GoalService. Later, more considered, and the code already agrees — goals_service.dart (292 lines) holds the transactional check-in and period rollover. This one is closed.

31 · Goal model vs a calendar-centric product DECIDED

A: explicit design axis: "Period-based progress (NOT scheduled timestamps)". Forge_Goal_Class_Design

B: the rest of the app is thoroughly timestamp-driven — events with startAt, day/multi/month calendar views, "events happening that day". Forge Initial Requirements, code

Resolution: both are correct for their own domain. A goal is a habit counter; an event is an appointment. Do not merge them into a hybrid — that is exactly the mushy middle to avoid. State the boundary once in the data model doc: goals never appear on the calendar grid, events never satisfy a check-in automatically.

32 · ResetPeriod.monthly DECIDED

A: an enum member named monthly, alongside daily/weekly/biweekly — implying a calendar month. Forge_Goal_Class_Design

B: implemented as a flat 30-day add. goals_service.dart _periodEnd

Resolution: the code is ground truth about what exists, not about what is right. A "monthly" goal that drifts five days a year will be reported as a bug by the first beta tester. Fix to a real calendar month. Cheap, contained.

33 · Goal history retention DECIDED

A: "at reset the current progress is stored to history and a new period begins automatically"; periodHistory feeds getOverallCompletionRate() and "some way that the user can see the entire checkup history". Forge_Goal_Class_Design, Goal Requirements #12

B: updateGoalForCurrentUser wipes periodHistory and resets currentCheckupsCompleted to 0 — renaming a goal destroys every period it ever recorded. Documented in-code as "Edit behavior (v1)". goals_service.dart

Resolution: spec wins; the code is a defect, not a decision. Silent destruction of user history on a rename is the kind of thing that ends a beta. T1 fix: only recompute periods when the period-defining fields actually change.

34 · What "private" means DECIDED

A: basic event: "set public or private view status" — visibility not scoped to anyone. Workout event: public = "all friends can view", private = "only invitees can view". Two different meanings in one document. Forge Initial Requirements

B: "different levels of visibility, including public or private" — implies three or more, none of them enumerated. Final Specs. Code: a single isPublic bool; rules grant read to owner, invitee, or (isPublic && (owner is my friend || owner is a gym I joined)). firestore.rules

Resolution: code wins: two levels, one meaning. "Public" means visible to your friends and to fellow members of gyms you belong to — never to the world. The Final Specs' third level was never defined and is dropped. Write this sentence once in the data model doc and stop paraphrasing it.

35 · Exercise / set schema DECIDED

A: "users can add reps and weight for each set"; "exercises, sets, reps, and weights defined during creation and edited later". A three-level hierarchy: workout → exercise → set{reps, weight}. Final Specs

B: the schema does not round-trip: the creator writes setDetails: [{reps, weight}] (event_creator_page.dart:1689) while _mapDocToEvent flattens to {name, sets, weight}, reading a top-level weight key that is never written — so the calendar always renders blank weights. And per-set editing is unbuilt: every set gets identical reps/weight (:1650).

Resolution: requirement stands; the code is broken. Two separate T1 fixes: repair the read path so weights display, and build per-set inputs. Note the doc says "edited later" — the edit sheet exists, so only the data shape is at fault.

36 · Plans DECIDED

A: no prose document anywhere describes Plans. Not in the requirements docs, not in the vision docs, not in the senior-project set.

B: a users/{uid}/plans/{planId} collection with its own rules, an events-by-planId composite index, and 496 + 658 lines of shipped service and UI. plans_service.dart, plan_creator_sheet.dart

Resolution: Plans exist and stay — they are the multi-week program concept the notes kept circling (record 23). Write the requirement retroactively. Known defects to carry: generation cap is 250 on create but 180 on update, plan lookup is by name (ambiguous with duplicates), and findPlanByNameForCurrentUser needs a name+updatedAt index that is not in firestore.indexes.json.

37 · Two competing repetition mechanisms DECIDED

A: events carry a recurrence map (weekly | monthly | every_other_day | custom_weekly | none) expanded client-side — one stored doc, N rendered occurrences. events_service.dart

B: Plans instead materialize real event docs, in batches chunked at 300, with an "update upcoming instances?" dialog. plans_service.dart

Resolution: keep both, but give each a job and write it down: recurrence = one repeating event (a standing Tuesday session); Plans = a multi-week program of different workouts. Without a stated boundary these will collide — a recurring event inside a plan would double-render. Neither is averaged away.

38 · Account-type detection DECIDED
