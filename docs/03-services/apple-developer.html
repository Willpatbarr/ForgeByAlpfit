Forge docs / Services / Apple Developer

Apple Developer Program DECIDED T1 Before first real user

What this is: the runbook for the $99/year Apple membership and TestFlight — the only legal way to get Forge onto an iPhone that isn't his. It is also the gate that surfaces the app's two hardest blockers: no moderation (guideline 1.2) and no account deletion (guideline 5.1.1(v)). Neither is paperwork; both are code that does not exist.

1. What & why

The Apple Developer Program is what lets Forge be signed, distributed to testers through TestFlight, and eventually submitted to the App Store. For this project its immediate value is TestFlight — the BYU-I Rec Center beta (D6) is the whole point of the next two milestones, and roughly half of a US campus population is on iPhone.

Alternative consideredOne-line reason it lost
Free provisioning (a personal Apple ID, sideload from Xcode)Builds expire after 7 days and you must physically plug in each device — fine for testing on his own phone, useless for a beta with strangers.
Android-only betaGenuinely cheaper ($25 vs $99+$25) and a legitimate fallback if money is tight — but it silently excludes about half the target population from a study about social behaviour, which is the thing being measured.
Apple Developer Enterprise Program$299/year, requires a D-U-N-S, and is for internal-only distribution within a company. Wrong tool.
Web app / PWA insteadD3 cuts web. A social workout app that can't push notifications isn't the product.

Why the paid program wins: TestFlight is the only mechanism that gets a build onto an arbitrary tester's iPhone without a cable, and $99/year is the entry price.

2. Adoption trigger

TierWhenWhy this tier
T1 Before first real user Enrol at M3, Ship to a device that isn't his. Enrolment takes 24–48 hours or longer, so start it a week before it's needed. This is the first genuinely committed money in the plan and the first hard external dependency. It is T1 rather than T0 because none of the app's real problems — open rules, hollow surfaces, zero tests — need $99 to fix.
Do NOT enrol yet — and this is a money argument, not a process one. The membership auto-renews annually and the clock starts the day you pay. Enrolling now buys a year that starts before there is anything to distribute. Wait until (a) the Firestore rules are fixed, (b) the bundle ID is real, and (c) there is an iOS build worth putting in a stranger's hands. Also note App Check's App Attest and APNs push both depend on this membership — so when it starts, several things unblock at once, and it's worth having them queued.
The real trigger is not the money — it's two missing features. Paying $99 does not get Forge onto TestFlight's external track, because that track requires Beta App Review, and review applies the App Review Guidelines. Forge currently fails two of them outright:
  • Guideline 1.2 (user-generated content) requires content filtering, a report mechanism with timely response, the ability to block abusive users, and published contact info. lib/services/moderation_service.dart is 0 bytes. There is no reporting, no blocking, no filtering — and isCurrentUserGymOwner is literally uid == gymUid, so a post's own author can't even delete their own post.
  • Guideline 5.1.1(v): "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." Forge's rules say delete: if false on /users and there is no UI. Account deletion is explicitly denied.
Both are weeks of real work. Enrolling before they are built means paying to be told no. Verify current guideline text at developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines.

4. Setup steps

Nothing here is done. iOS signing and team are not configured in project.pbxproj, and the bundle ID is still the Flutter template default.

  1. Decide the bundle ID before anything else. Current value is com.example.forgeSeniorProjectVersion — Apple rejects com.example.*. PROPOSED com.alpfit.forge: reverse-DNS on the parent brand, leaves room for future AlpFit apps, and drops the dead "senior project" wording. Confirm the choice with William before typing it anywhere — see the callout below for why.
  2. Enrol at developer.apple.com/programs/enroll as an Individual. Requires a real Apple ID with two-factor auth and a payment method. Allow 24–48 hours or more for approval. Note: enrolling as an Individual means the App Store seller name is his legal name, not "AlpFit" — switching to an Organization later requires a D-U-N-S and is a separate enrolment.
  3. Create the App ID (Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles → Identifiers) with the chosen bundle ID, and enable the capabilities Forge will need: Push Notifications (for FCM) and App Attest (for App Check). Enabling them now avoids a second pass.
  4. Update the Xcode project: set PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER to the new ID, set the Team, and change the display name from Forge Senior Project Version and CFBundleName from forge_senior_project_version to Forge. Also remove the landscape orientations from Info.plist — the app declares landscape support and no screen has a landscape layout, which is a visible review risk.
  5. Re-register the iOS app in Firebase under the new bundle ID and regenerate GoogleService-Info.plist and firebase_options.dart. Doing this before App Check and push is what keeps this from being a two-pass job.
  6. Create the app record in App Store Connect — name, primary language, bundle ID, and SKU. The SKU can never be changed after the app is added.
  7. Fill in App Privacy (the "nutrition label" questionnaire) — required to submit. Forge collects email, display name, contact info, avatars, user content, and (once adopted) crash data. Answer honestly; a mismatch between the label and actual behaviour is itself a rejection reason.
  8. Provide a privacy policy URL — required for all apps, and Forge currently has no privacy policy anywhere in the repo or the UI. This is what Cloudflare Pages is for.
  9. Declare EU Digital Services Act trader status. Required for every developer, including ones not distributing in the EU — Apple's help page is explicit that you must declare even if you don't. New submissions are blocked until it's done. Apple's guidance.
  10. Build the two blockers — in-app account deletion (5.1.1(v)) and report/block/filter moderation (1.2). Apple requires deletion to be initiated in-app; a web link may only complete the flow, never replace the in-app entry point, and it must delete the account record rather than deactivate it. This is the long pole in the whole milestone.
  11. Archive and upload a build via Xcode or flutter build ipa + Transporter. Once a build is uploaded, the bundle ID is frozen forever.
  12. Add internal testers, install from TestFlight, confirm it runs. Then create an external group and submit for Beta App Review.
  13. Verify: a person who is not him, on their own iPhone, installs Forge from a TestFlight link, registers a new account, and creates a workout event — with no cable, no Xcode, and no help. That end-to-end run is the only thing that proves this service is set up. "The build uploaded" is not the same claim.
Painful to change later — the permanent list. Bundle ID (Apple: "You can't change this property after you upload a build") · SKU (can't change after the app is added) · Apple ID / seller identity (individual vs organization is a re-enrolment) · Team ID (baked into the APNs key association). The app name is more forgiving — editable until submitted for review, then changeable with a new version. Get the bundle ID right in one pass: it also has to match Firebase, App Check's App Attest registration, and the APNs key. Changing it after upload means a new App ID, a new Firebase app registration, a new App Check setup, and a re-uploaded APNs configuration.

Add *.p8, *.p12, *.mobileprovision, and *.cer to .gitignore before enrolling, not after; the GitHub runbook lists this as a step for exactly this reason. The two .p8 files are downloadable exactly once. If either is lost, revoke it in the portal and generate a new one — for APNs that means re-uploading to Firebase and push breaks until you do. Put both in a password manager the moment they touch disk, and delete them from Downloads.

6. Escape hatch

Lock-in: MEDIUM — not technical lock-in so much as a gatekeeper you can't route around.

QuestionAnswer
What data lives only here?The app record, TestFlight tester emails and their feedback, App Store reviews and ratings, and sales/analytics reports. Notably, no user account data — that all lives in Firebase. Losing the Apple account loses distribution, not the product.
How do I export?TestFlight tester lists export from App Store Connect. Reviews and analytics come out via the App Store Connect API. The app is your own code and needs no export.
What does leaving cost?iOS distribution, entirely. There is no alternative iOS channel in the US; sideloading and alternative marketplaces exist only in the EU under the DMA and are not a realistic path for this project. Leaving Apple means shipping Android-only.
What migrating within Apple costsMoving from an Individual to an Organization account is a fresh enrolment plus a D-U-N-S, and app transfer between accounts is possible but has conditions. If "AlpFit" is meant to be a company name on the App Store eventually, that's worth thinking about before enrolling, not after.
The reversible partThe Flutter codebase itself is untouched by any of this. Nothing in lib/ knows Apple exists apart from the platform channel plugins.

7. Cost & scale checkpoints

ScaleCostWhat breaks
~10 users$99/yr (~$8.25/mo)Nothing. Internal TestFlight (100 tester cap) covers this entirely with no Beta App Review at all — the cheapest, fastest path for the first handful of people.
~100 users (the Rec Center beta)$99/yr — unchangedCost doesn't move; TestFlight's 10,000 external cap isn't near. What breaks is process: this scale needs the external track, which means Beta App Review, which means guidelines 1.2 and 5.1.1(v) are enforced. The 90-day build expiry also becomes real — a semester-long beta needs a re-upload partway through, and forgetting means every tester's app stops opening at once.
~1,000 users$99/yr — still unchangedApple's cost is flat at every scale (no monetization, so no commission). Still inside TestFlight's 10,000 external cap. At this point the pressure moves to full App Store review rather than Beta App Review, which is stricter — and to review-response obligations under guideline 1.2, which requires timely responses to reports, i.e. someone actually reading a moderation queue. That's a time cost, and it's the one nobody budgets for.

8. Teammate delta

QuestionAnswer
Extra seat costApple publishes no per-seat fee for adding App Store Connect users — but "it's free" is unverified; no Apple page states it affirmatively. Treat it as almost certainly free and confirm in the portal.
Does the program allow another member?Yes. App Store Connect supports multiple users with roles. Note that an Individual enrolment is a personal account — team management exists but the legal entity is still him.
Permission modelRoles: Account Holder, Admin, App Manager, Developer, Marketing, Sales, Finance, Customer Support. There is exactly one Account Holder and it cannot be shared. Give a collaborating developer the Developer role — enough to upload builds, not enough to change agreements or banking. Apple's role reference.
What actually changesThree frictions arrive together: (1) certificate sharing — the distribution certificate lives in one person's Keychain, and the standard fix is Xcode's automatic cloud-managed signing or a fastlane match repo; (2) device provisioning for anyone testing push, since iOS push doesn't work in the simulator; (3) role gates — creating or revoking the APNs key needs Account Holder or Admin, so a Developer-role teammate will hit a wall doing push setup.
Internal testersEvery App Store Connect user with Account Holder, Admin, App Manager, Developer, or Marketing can be a TestFlight internal tester at no extra cost — up to 100.

What to do next

Do not enrol yet. Decide the bundle ID instead — confirm com.alpfit.forge (or pick another) and change it everywhere before a single build is uploaded, because after that it is permanent and it also pins Firebase, App Check, and APNs.
  1. Then scope the two blockers honestly: in-app account deletion (5.1.1(v)) and report/block moderation (1.2). Both are code that does not exist, and both gate external TestFlight — not just the App Store.
  2. Enrol about a week before M3, since approval takes 24–48 hours or more and the annual clock starts on payment.
  3. Add *.p8, *.p12, *.mobileprovision to .gitignore before any key exists to forget about.
