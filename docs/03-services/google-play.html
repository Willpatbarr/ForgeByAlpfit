Google Play Console DECIDED T1 Before first real user

Runbook for Google Play registration, testing, and release requirements.

1. What & why

Play Console is how Forge gets onto Android phones — first through the internal testing track (100 testers, builds live in minutes, minimal review), then eventually production. For the Rec Center beta it is the cheaper and faster half of the two stores: $25 once instead of $99/year, and internal-track builds appear in minutes instead of waiting on a review queue.

Alternatives considered, and why each lost:

  • Sideloading APKs directly to testers — free and it does technically work, but it means asking gym members to enable "install unknown apps", it gives no update mechanism, and it makes a fitness app look like malware to the exact strangers you're trying to win over.
  • Firebase App Distribution — genuinely good for pre-store builds and free, but it does not lead anywhere. Play Console is needed eventually regardless, and Play Integrity for App Check is linked through it, so paying the $25 once collapses two steps into one.
  • Amazon Appstore / F-Droid — wrong audience entirely for a US campus gym.
  • Android-only, skip Apple — a real budget option (see Apple), but it excludes roughly half the target population from a study about social behaviour.

Why Play wins: $25 one-time is the cheapest real distribution channel in the plan, and it is a prerequisite for Play Integrity anyway.

2. Adoption trigger

Tier: T1 Before first real user

Trigger: register at M3, Ship to a device that isn't his — and register early within M3, because of the 14-day clock below.

Why this tier: $25 is small enough that the cost isn't the gate; the time-based requirements are. Registering early costs nothing extra and starts clocks that cannot be compressed later.

The 12 × 14 rule — the deadline that will surprise him. Personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for at least 14 consecutive days before they can even apply for production access. Production review then typically takes up to 7 days. Google's requirements page.
  • The 14 days are consecutive, and testers must stay opted in — someone leaving mid-window restarts the clock.
  • Practical consequence: from a standing start, public Android launch is a minimum of ~3 weeks away, no matter how finished the app is. Register and run the closed test in parallel with development, not after it.
  • Applicability to organization accounts is unverified — Google's page scopes the rule to personal accounts and is silent on orgs.
  • The reported reduction from 20 testers to 12 (Dec 2024) comes from third-party sources — re-check the current number in Play Console before counting on 12.
Good news: the internal testing track (100 testers) has none of this. For the Rec Center beta, internal testing is the right track and the 12×14 rule does not apply.
The target-API deadline, which is live right now. As of 31 August 2026, new apps and updates must target Android 16 / API 36 or higher, with an extension available to 1 November 2026. Forge's android/app/build.gradle.kts sets targetSdk = flutter.targetSdkVersion — unpinned, whatever the toolchain happens to default to. Pin it explicitly and verify the value at developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk, because "whatever Flutter defaults to" is not a plan you can audit.
When NOT to set this up yet: not before the Firestore rules are fixed, and not before the package name is real. Registering the account is harmless and can happen early; creating the app entry should wait until the package name is final, because it freezes on first publish.

4. Setup steps

Nothing here is done. The release build is debug-signed, the package name is the Flutter template default, and the app label is the raw project slug.

  1. Decide the package name. Current: com.example.forge_senior_project_version — Play rejects com.example.* outright. PROPOSED com.alpfit.forge, matching the iOS bundle ID for consistency. Confirm with William before it is typed anywhere. Change applicationId and namespace in android/app/build.gradle.kts, and move the Kotlin source directory to match.
  2. Register at play.google.com/console/signup, pay $25, choose a Personal account. Complete identity verification — Google requires it, and personal accounts also verify Android device access through the Play Console mobile app. Do this early; verification is not instant.
  3. Fix the app label. AndroidManifest.xml currently shows forge_senior_project_version on the home screen. Change it to Forge.
  4. Pin the SDK levels explicitly in build.gradle.kts — minSdk, targetSdk, compileSdk are all inherited from flutter.* today, so nobody can state what the app actually supports. Set targetSdk to the level Play currently requires (see the deadline callout) and pick a deliberate minSdk floor.
  5. Create the upload keystore and a real signing config. The release block currently reads signingConfig = signingConfigs.getByName("debug") — a debug-signed AAB cannot be uploaded to Play at all. Generate an RSA ≥2048 key into a .jks, put the credentials in android/key.properties, and reference it from a proper release signing config. Back the keystore up somewhere you will still have in five years.
  6. Add the permissions the app actually uses. AndroidManifest.xml declares none beyond the Flutter defaults despite using image_picker, and push needs POST_NOTIFICATIONS on Android 13+.
  7. Re-register the Android app in Firebase under the new package name; regenerate google-services.json and firebase_options.dart. Add the release key's SHA-256 fingerprint to the Firebase Android app — App Check / Play Integrity needs it.
  8. Create the app in Play Console. Play App Signing enrols new apps automatically with a Google-managed signing key; your .jks becomes the upload key. The package name freezes on first publish.
  9. Complete the App content section: privacy policy URL (hosted on Cloudflare Pages), Data safety form, content rating questionnaire, target audience, and ads declaration. Data safety must honestly cover email, display name, contact info, avatars, user content, and crash data.
  10. Satisfy the UGC policy. Play requires an in-app system for reporting and blocking objectionable content and users, plus terms of use the user accepts. moderation_service.dart is 0 bytes. Same blocker as Apple guideline 1.2 — build it once, satisfy both. Play UGC policy.
  11. Satisfy the account-deletion policy. Play requires both an in-app path to delete the account and its data, and a web URL where deletion can be requested — declared in Data safety and shown on the store listing. Note the difference from Apple: Apple wants it initiated in-app; Google wants in-app and a public web page. That web page is a concrete deliverable for Cloudflare Pages. Play deletion policy.
  12. Build and upload an AAB: flutter build appbundle --release, then upload to the Internal testing track and add testers by email or list.
  13. Verify: a person who is not him installs Forge from the Play internal-testing link on their own Android phone, registers, and creates a workout event — no ADB, no sideloading, no help. Separately confirm in Play Console that the upload was accepted with the release signing key, not a debug one. Both, not just the first.
Painful to change later — the permanent list. Package name (Android's own docs: don't change it after publishing, because Play treats a new one as an entirely new app — new listing, zero installs, zero reviews) · the upload keystore (lose it and you can never ship an update; Play App Signing makes upload-key reset possible but it is a support process, not a checkbox) · the Google account that owns the console · the developer name shown on the listing. Get the package name right in one pass — it also pins Firebase registration and App Check.

Never commit a real secret. Add *.jks, *.keystore, and android/key.properties to .gitignore before generating the keystore — the stock Flutter .gitignore in this repo does not cover them, and a committed keystore in a repo that may be public is a genuine compromise. Commit .env.example with the empty names above.

The backup rule, stated once and bluntly: the .jks is not like other secrets. Leaking it is bad; losing it is worse — a lost signing key with no Play App Signing reset means the app can never be updated again and users must reinstall a new listing from scratch. Two copies, two places, today.

6. Escape hatch

Lock-in: MEDIUM — lower than Apple's, because Android has real alternative channels.

  • What data lives only here — the store listing, install and rating statistics, tester lists, crash/ANR data (Android vitals), and the Google-managed app signing key. No user account data; that is all in Firebase.
  • How to export — statistics and reviews export as CSV or via the Play Developer API. Listing text and assets are your own. Under Play App Signing the app signing key is held by Google and is not exportable — that's the sticky bit.
  • What leaving costs — genuinely low. An AAB/APK can be distributed via Amazon Appstore, F-Droid, Samsung Galaxy Store, or direct download. You'd lose the install base and reviews, and users would need to reinstall from the new source, but Android does not forbid the exit the way iOS does.
  • What's actually sticky — the package name (a new one is a new app with zero history) and Play App Signing key custody. Both are recoverable-with-pain rather than fatal.
  • The $25 — non-refundable and one-time. It is a sunk cost small enough not to influence any decision.

7. Cost & scale checkpoints

ScaleCostWhat breaks
~10 users$25 once, then $0/moNothing — internal testing covers this entirely
~100 users (the Rec Center beta)$0/mo — the $25 is already spentThe 100-tester internal cap lands exactly here
~1,000 users$0/mo — Play's cost is flat and always wasAndroid vitals thresholds and moderation response time
  • ~10 users — internal testing's 100 testers means builds live in minutes with no policy review gate. The cheapest, fastest distribution in the entire plan.
  • ~100 users — a beta that grows past 100 must move to closed or open testing, and closed testing is where the 12×14 rule lives. Plan that transition before you need it, because it is a 14-day minimum, not a button. Play policy review also becomes real at that point: the UGC and account-deletion requirements stop being theoretical.
  • ~1,000 users — still no cost (no monetization, so no commission). Two non-cost pressures instead: Android vitals thresholds, since Play demotes apps with bad crash and ANR rates in search rankings, which makes Crashlytics and the known unchecked-cast crashes a distribution problem rather than just a quality one; and moderation response time, since Play's UGC policy expects reports to actually be acted on.

8. Teammate delta

QuestionAnswer
Extra seat cost$0 — confirmed. Only the account owner ever pays
Does the free tier allow another member?Yes, with no documented cap on users
Permission modelTwo scopes: account-level and app-level
What actually changesThe keystore becomes a shared secret

  • Extra seat cost — Google's docs state an added user "doesn't need to pay the $25 registration fee". Play user permissions.
  • Permission model — account-level permissions apply across all apps (creating draft apps is account-only) and app-level permissions apply to one app. Genuinely granular: you can grant "view app information" and "manage testing tracks" without granting release-to-production. Better than Apple's fixed role list for this purpose.
  • What actually changes — a shared keystore is the moment it most often leaks. Do not email it. Either keep releases with one person, or put the base64 keystore in a GitHub Actions secret and have CI sign, which is the T2 release-automation work arriving early because a second person forced it.
  • Note vs Apple — onboarding a second Android developer is dramatically easier than the iOS equivalent.

What to do next

Replace the debug signing config with a real release keystore, and back that keystore up in two places — everything else on this page is blocked behind an AAB that Play will actually accept, and a lost keystore is the one mistake here with no recovery.
  1. Confirm the package name (com.alpfit.forge proposed) and change applicationId + namespace before creating the Play app entry — it freezes on first publish.
  2. Pin minSdk / targetSdk / compileSdk explicitly and check the currently-required target API — the API 36 requirement lands 31 August 2026.
  3. Register the $25 account early. Identity verification and the 12×14 closed-testing clock are time-based and cannot be compressed later.
  4. Add *.jks and android/key.properties to .gitignore before generating anything.
